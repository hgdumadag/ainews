⚡ 30-Second Executive Summary

Enterprise AI has hit a strategic inflection point this week: OpenAI declared enterprise adoption "at a tipping point," simultaneously launching a $4 billion deployment company, while the EU agreed on May 7 to extend high-risk AI compliance deadlines to December 2027 — giving organizations more runway but no reason to pause governance work. The biggest business opportunity lies in governed agentic AI, with ServiceNow's new platform offering auditable, policy-bound automation across operations, finance, and IT for organizations that act now. The gravest risk is a compounding threat cluster: deepfake voice fraud averaging $680,000 per incident, a US court ruling that public AI tools waive attorney-client privilege, and new NTT DATA research showing 95% of enterprises recognize AI sovereignty risks but only 29% are acting on them. Audit leadership should prioritize three questions at the next committee meeting: which AI use cases are live without a formal governance inventory, do vendor contracts address AI data use and liability, and are controls ready for the EU AI Act's August 2026 transparency obligations?

🔭 Executive Watchlist
  • Monitor OpenAI Deployment Company ramp-up: Watch for client announcements, pricing disclosures, and contract terms emerging from the new $4B enterprise venture. This will signal how aggressively OpenAI plans to compete with existing system integrators and managed service providers.
  • Discuss EU AI Act August 2026 transparency obligations: While high-risk AI deadlines are extended to December 2027, chatbot transparency and AI-generated content labeling obligations still apply in August 2026. Boards should confirm whether any customer-facing AI tools require disclosure updates before that date.
  • Act Deepfake/voice fraud controls gap: With average losses of $680K per incident and voice samples now widely stolen, enterprises should urgently review verification protocols for financial authorization requests, supplier payments, and executive impersonation scenarios — particularly in finance and treasury.
  • Audit Question AI use inventory: Can internal audit confirm a complete and current inventory of AI tools in use across the enterprise — including tools adopted by business units without formal IT or governance approval? What percentage have undergone risk assessment?
  • Governance Vendor contract AI clauses: Assess whether existing technology vendor contracts include provisions addressing AI data use, model training on company data, confidentiality, liability for AI-generated errors, and audit rights. Contracts signed before 2025 almost certainly lack these clauses.
❓ Questions for Management
  1. Which AI use cases are currently operating across JG Summit businesses without a formal risk assessment, governance review, or inclusion in the AI inventory — and what is the plan to remediate this gap within the next 90 days?
  2. Do our vendor contracts — especially with cloud, SaaS, and AI platform providers — explicitly address AI data use, confidentiality obligations, prohibition on training AI models on our data, liability for AI-generated errors, and audit rights?
  3. Are finance, treasury, and procurement teams aware of the escalating deepfake voice fraud risk, and have out-of-band verification protocols been established for high-value payment authorizations and executive-directed transfers?
  4. What is our exposure under the EU AI Act's August 2026 transparency obligations? Which customer-facing AI systems interact with EU users and require disclosure or labeling updates before the August deadline?
  5. Are we measuring AI productivity gains against actual business outcomes — revenue uplift, cost reduction, cycle time, error rate — rather than adoption metrics alone, and who is accountable for reporting these results to leadership?
