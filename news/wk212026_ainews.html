⚡ 30-Second Executive Summary

The week's most critical regulatory signal is the EU's Digital Omnibus agreement extending AI Act high-risk compliance deadlines to 2027–2028 — providing breathing room but making enforcement inevitable, and adding a new ban on non-consensual AI-generated intimate content. The most urgent business opportunity is the restructured OpenAI-Microsoft partnership opening genuine multi-cloud AI options, while the Cohere-Aleph Alpha merger creates a credible sovereign AI alternative for regulated industries seeking data control. The dominant risk thread is the acceleration of AI-enabled deepfake and voice-cloning fraud — enterprises suffered $1.1 billion in corporate account losses in 2025, compounded by a US federal court ruling that using consumer AI tools can permanently waive attorney-client privilege. Executive and audit leadership should immediately discuss AI fraud controls, enterprise tool policies, and governance assurance frameworks before the EU enforcement window closes and before the next deepfake incident strikes.

👁 Executive Watchlist
  • Monitor: EU AI Act Digital Omnibus implementation — while deadlines are extended to 2027–2028, provisionally agreed provisions on new prohibitions take effect December 2026. Begin gap assessment now to avoid last-minute compliance pressure.
  • Discuss: AI fraud control framework — the $1.1B in 2025 corporate deepfake losses and the 1,600% surge in AI-enabled voice phishing demand a board-level conversation on multi-layered verification controls for high-value transactions.
  • Act: Review enterprise AI tool policy — the February 2026 US federal court ruling that consumer AI use can waive attorney-client privilege creates immediate legal exposure. GC should audit which employees are using which tools and update acceptable-use policies within 60 days.
  • Audit/Risk Question: Does the organization have a complete inventory of all AI use cases, including unsanctioned "shadow AI" deployments, and are each of these assessed for data-handling, privacy, and regulatory compliance risk?
  • Governance Update: Adopt AI board governance principles — the April 2026 KPMG/INSEAD framework covering strategy, security, workforce, trustworthy AI, and board oversight processes provides a ready-made structure for embedding AI governance into the board's oversight mandate.
❓ Questions for Management
  1. AI Use Case Inventory: Which AI use cases are currently operating across the business — including vendor-embedded AI — and have all been formally inventoried, risk-assessed, and assigned a responsible business owner?
  2. Deepfake Fraud Controls: What verification procedures exist for high-value wire transfers, vendor payment changes, or executive-instruction calls? Have these been stress-tested against AI-enabled voice-cloning and deepfake scenarios?
  3. Enterprise Tool Policy: Do all employees and legal staff understand that using consumer AI tools (free or personal-tier ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Copilot) with privileged or confidential information may permanently waive legal protection? Is there a written policy enforced via training?
  4. EU AI Act Readiness: Do any of the organization's AI applications qualify as "high-risk" under the EU AI Act (e.g., AI used in employment decisions, credit assessments, or customer-facing decisioning)? Has a readiness gap assessment been commissioned?
  5. AI ROI Measurement: How is the organization measuring the business value of AI investments — specifically whether productivity gains are translating to revenue growth, cost reduction, or margin improvement — and what is the governance process for continuing or cancelling underperforming AI projects?
