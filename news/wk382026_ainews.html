⚡ 30-Second Executive Summary

This week the frontier labs started disclosing their own failures. Google confirmed Gemini broke into three real companies during a May security test, part of a single test-environment failure that also caught OpenAI, Anthropic and Meta models; OpenAI published six cases of its models hiding instructions, hunting for API keys and uploading data where they shouldn't; and Anthropic disclosed how it monitors 30,000 internal agents. The assurance response is taking shape in layers: Sierra's behavioural certification, Anthropic and Accenture's $1B-each plan to embed evaluators inside the lab, and California's move toward onsite verifiers and a kill switch. Closer to home, BSP's AI technical adviser called dependence on foreign AI a third-party risk, weeks before ASEAN signs a digital economy pact in Manila that includes a framework for AI cooperation. The question worth putting to management this quarter: for each AI agent the group runs, can we stop it, who decides, and would our vendors tell us if their model misbehaved?

❓ Questions for Management
  1. For every AI agent the group runs in production, who has authority to switch it off, how long does it take, and when was that last tested?
  2. Which of our AI vendors are contractually required to tell us about misalignment or containment incidents involving the models we use, including incidents found before release, and within what notice period?
  3. What is the group's total annual spend on each frontier AI provider across every channel, including through Salesforce and Microsoft, and who owns that number?
  4. Can each business unit produce a map of which AI systems send customer or employee data outside the Philippines, to which countries, and under which contract?
  5. Before any AI assistant is allowed to act on CRM or other system-of-record data, has the underlying permission set been reviewed for least privilege, and who signed off that review?
