⚡ 30-Second Executive Summary

This week's defining signal is that AI governance moved from principle to machinery. California created the first registry of licensed AI auditors and a framework for independent verification, ISACA named the four gaps conventional IT audit leaves open on autonomous agents, and Salesforce bid to own the audit trail of AI actions across vendors it does not control. Two things landed alongside it that the committee will be asked about: an Anthropic researcher resigned publicly saying the labs are gambling with our lives, with Anthropic's own alignment lead putting extinction risk above 10% within the decade; and OpenAI's GPT-6 Astra, released September 3 and therefore outside this window, became the first model the company rates Critical for cyber capability. The clearest opportunity is cost: OpenAI priced a full-duplex voice agent layer at $0.05 per minute, making automation of airline reservations, retail service and banking queues economically obvious for the first time. The sharpest risk is that connectors, not models, are the exfiltration route — Check Point pulled a victim's Gmail data out of ChatGPT through a shared internal service. The discussion worth having this quarter: who in the group holds the authority to enable a new AI model or agent against bank, HR and customer data, and what evidence we retain when they do.

📌 Executive Watchlist — next 30 to 90 days
🔵
MonitorGovernor Newsom has until September 30 to act on more than thirty remaining California AI bills, including chatbot disclosure and a 90-day notice requirement for AI-driven displacement affecting 25% or more of a workforce. These are the provisions most likely to be copied elsewhere.
🟡
DiscussOpenAI says the less-restricted cyber capabilities in GPT-6 Astra go only to vetted organisations through its Daybreak programme, expanding in the coming weeks. Whether our security function can obtain defensive tooling that attackers are already using without restriction is a Q4 procurement question, not an IT one.
🔴
ActRotate every AI service credential and API key in the group within 90 days. Two independent sources this week, Check Point's ChatGPT disclosure and Anthropic's threat report, both point to connectors and stolen keys as the live exfiltration route rather than model flaws.
🟣
Audit / assuranceEstablish the agent evidence standard covering prompt, model version, permissions, tool calls, approvals and rollback before the first agent goes live in any business unit. ISACA's guidance is the reference, and retrofitting this after deployment is materially more expensive.
🩵
Policy / governanceDraft the model-approval matrix for Microsoft 365 Copilot: which models may process bank, HR and customer data, who approves each case, and what is logged. The Grok toggle is a single administrative click with group-wide consequences.
❓ Questions for Management
  1. Who in the group currently holds the authority to enable a new AI model inside Microsoft 365 or any other production platform, what evidence is retained when they exercise it, and is that decision right documented anywhere outside IT?
  2. Which AI connectors have read access to banking, HR, legal or audit repositories today, who approved each one, and when was that authorisation last reviewed?
  3. What percentage of our AI service credentials and API keys have been rotated in the last 90 days, and can our monitoring detect AI-initiated network activity as a distinct pattern, yes or no?
  4. For any agent authorised to take a monetary or customer-affecting action, how is human confirmation recorded, how quickly can a completed action be reversed, and who is accountable when it cannot be?
  5. What is the group's total contractual exposure to multi-year AI infrastructure and cloud commitments, including prepayments made directly by business units, and who reviews those terms centrally before signature?
